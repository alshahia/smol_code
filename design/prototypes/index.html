Design review · 24 October 2026

Three rooms. One decision.

Cuebook is still the benchmark. Beside it are two directions built from scratch for this product — not re-skins of the rejected worlds. Same screens, same data, same product truth: the tier ladder, the release gate, the audit chain, the composer. Only the room changes.

What is fixed. Tier names, run states, the approval decision, the audit register, and the behavior of every control. A direction may rename a surface in its own language, never the destination or the action.

What is new. Custody comes from an evidence intake bench; Bonded from a customs yard. Both are invented from the operator's world, not from the instruments the operator uses.

What is not real. No backend, no keys, all runs and costs are synthetic. Open the nav, open both release sheets, check the register, narrow the window to 390px.

What actually differs.

The product is identical. The world changes how quickly you read state, where the decision sits, and what the tool feels like when nothing is happening.

How to choose.

  1. Open all three live runs. Leave each one open for a minute with the release closed, then open it.
  2. Find the decision. How long did it take you to see that something needs your signature — and did the world make it feel consequential?
  3. Read the register. The audit trail is the product's promise; whichever register you would actually verify is the honest answer.
  4. Pick the room, not the screenshot. The chosen direction will be rebuilt in React against the real components — density, keyboard, and states included.

My read: Cuebook still has the strongest identity and the cleanest emotional read of the gate. Custody is the most honest to the product: it makes custody, seal, and register into the interface rather than decoration. Bonded is the most legible at a distance and gives the queue a physical place. None of them is a re-skin; the rejected worlds are archived on disk, not linked here.

What happens after.

Pick one and it becomes the design contract: tokens, type scale, spacing, component shapes, and the one signature move get written down, then applied to the React app in smolcode/web. The other two are archived as alternatives, not merged.

If none of them is right, say what is wrong with each — the useful note is usually “too theatrical”, “too clinical”, or “too much metaphor” — and I will roll again from the same product truth.

Deliberately not included: provider API-key entry, upload drag-and-drop, and the MCP tool browser. Those are real product surfaces, but they would have been three more screens of the same argument in each world. They come back in the chosen build.
